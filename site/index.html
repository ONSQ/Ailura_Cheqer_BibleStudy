The tools of the search

Everything a word carries

Word anatomy

Where the word comes from, the family of words built on its root, how the lexicon lays out its senses, and the forms the text actually uses: Qal, Niphal, Piel; aorist, participle. From Brown-Driver-Briggs and Abbott-Smith, never from guesswork.

Every occurrence

All 2,603 places אֱלֹהִים appears, in canonical order, each one a tap away from the verse. Counts show the base text beside the amalgamated total, so nothing is hidden.

How translators render it

Every English gloss with its count, and a view of how a word's dominant sense moves from the Torah to the Prophets to the Writings.

Ask in plain English

“Where does Scripture speak of the sons of God?” The answer is searched from the text, not remembered, and every verse it cites was retrieved first. Ask about one verse, or about the whole Bible.

Who & where

Twenty-nine Zechariahs, three Herods. Tap a name and see which one, with family, name forms, a map for places, and every appearance.

Your studies

Save a word, a question, or an answer. Keep it private or publish it for every reader. Share any of it to Messages, mail or wherever you talk.

The deeper counsel

Sod סוֹד

“For who has stood in the council of the LORD to see and to hear His word?” (Jeremiah 23:18). Sod is the intimate counsel, the inner circle where a matter is searched out. In Cheqer it is the panel that opens a word to the witnesses of its own age.

The Old and New Testaments are tagged to the last word, so finding a word there is exact. The world around them is not: Josephus and Philo, 1 Enoch and Jubilees, the Testaments of the Twelve Patriarchs, the Septuagint and the Targum, and soon the Dead Sea Scrolls. Cheqer reads those too, and finds the passages whose subject is closest to the word you are studying.

Then it writes a word-study brief: the word taken apart, its path from the Torah through the Prophets, how the Greek translators handled it, what the Second Temple writers made of the idea, and where it surfaces in the New Testament. Every claim carries a citation back to a text you can open. The brief summarizes witnesses; it never invents doctrine.

  • Septuagint · the Greek Old Testament, with Brenton's English
  • Targum Onkelos · the Aramaic Torah
  • Josephus · Antiquities and War, Greek and Whiston
  • Philo · the Alexandrian commentaries, Greek and Yonge
  • 1 Enoch, Jubilees, Ben Sira, the Testaments, the Maccabees
  • Dead Sea Scrolls · on the way
